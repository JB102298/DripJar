GOAL

Build the accounting foundation DripJar will use for real financial activity later.

This phase must establish:

1. An auditable double-entry ledger.
2. A financial transaction model that separates:
   - Jar contribution principal
   - DripJar 3% service fee
   - payment-processing fee
   - total customer charge
3. Contributor-level refundable / committed / refunded principal accounting.
4. Immutable ledger history with compensating entries instead of editing money history.
5. Server-owned fee calculation.
6. Destination abstraction for future organizer bank payout vs DripJar Card.
7. Financial invariants and concurrency protections.
8. ZERO payment-provider calls in this phase.

BUSINESS RULES

Treat these as fixed product rules for Phase 4A:

A. A contributor chooses how much principal they want to Drip into the Jar.

Example:
Jar contribution principal = $200.00

B. DripJar charges a 3% service fee on that principal.

$200.00 × 3% = $6.00

C. Payment-processing fees are separate.

The future provider fee must NOT reduce Jar principal.

D. Customer-facing conceptual total:

Jar contribution principal
+ DripJar fee
+ payment-processing fee
=
total customer charge

E. The full intended principal counts toward the Jar.

If the user Drips $200 principal, the Jar receives/credits $200 principal.

F. DripJar's 3% fee is non-refundable.

G. Future payment-processing fees are non-refundable from DripJar.

H. Contribution principal remains refundable until that contributor explicitly commits it.

I. After explicit commitment, committed principal becomes non-refundable under the Jar agreement.

J. Reaching the cutoff date by itself does NOT commit real funds.

K. Every contributor commits their own principal individually.

L. Organizer cannot commit another contributor's funds.

M. All money values use integer cents only.

PART 3 — DOUBLE-ENTRY LEDGER

Implement a proper immutable double-entry ledger.

Preferred architecture:

ledger_accounts
ledger_transactions
ledger_entries

or equivalent.

Requirements:

1. Every posted financial transaction must balance.
2. Sum of debits and credits for a ledger transaction must net to zero.
3. Ledger entries cannot be mutated after posting.
4. Corrections must use reversing/compensating entries.
5. No delete endpoint for posted ledger entries.
6. Use integer cents only.
7. Store currency explicitly.
8. Link ledger transactions to financial_transactions where applicable.
9. Include timestamps and immutable references.

Design account types sufficient for DripJar's future model.

At minimum consider accounts representing:

- external payment clearing
- contributor refundable principal
- Jar committed principal
- DripJar service-fee revenue
- processing-fee pass-through / clearing
- refund clearing
- future payout/card clearing

Choose coherent accounting semantics and document them.
